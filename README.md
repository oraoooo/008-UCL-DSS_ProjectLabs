Did the Elizabeth line make house prices rise near its stations?

Question: Since the line opened in May 2022, have prices near its stations risen faster than prices in similar areas without one?
Data: Land Registry sale prices (every sale with its postcode) plus station locations, joined using a postcode lookup that gives each postcode its coordinates.
Your twist: use hexagons to measure price growth by distance from the nearest station. You’d get a map showing how far the effect reaches, which few teams will be able to make.
Why it’s strong: it has a clear before and after, a natural comparison group, and an impact everyone understands. There’s also a good twist to test: did prices jump when the line opened, or years earlier when it was announced?
Watch out:
Prices rose across London over the same period, so always compare against areas with no new station.
The full sales file is large, so download one year at a time and keep only London rows.
